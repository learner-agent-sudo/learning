# Hong Kong law facts for "Open source & software IP" (the Hong Kong tab)

Checked against the current Hong Kong e-Legislation text held in data/hkel (law.py), licence texts in data/licences, and
the fact-checked Hong Kong banking volume for HKMA guidance. [V]: supported by the held text (claims marked P were
corrected to what the text says). [R]: not in held text: a case, guidance, practice or proposal; state it with

## map
- [R] **map-1** Hong Kong is a separate IP jurisdiction from Mainland China, with its own ordinances and its own registries (Patents, Designs, Trade Marks, Copyright). The constitutional basis for that separation (Basic Law Arts 139-140) is not in the held texts. To check: Not checkable from held text. The held ordinances show separate HK registries (Cap. 522 s.2 defines the Designs Registry; Cap. 514 and Cap. 559 are HK ordinances), but the separation from Mainland law needs the Basic Law Arts 139-140 text and any Mainland-HK IP arrangements, which were not held.
- [V] **map-2** Copyright does not need an application or registration. A literary work (including a computer program) is protected only once it is recorded in writing or otherwise, and the time of making is the time of recording. The Copyright Ordinance has no register of works; its only register is of copyright licensing bodies (ss.146-147). [cites: Cap. 528 2; Cap. 528 4; Cap. 528 147; s.2(1)(a) (original works); s.4(2)-(3) (recording requirement); s.147(1) (register of copyright licensing bodies only).]
- [V] **map-3** Hong Kong has three patent routes. A standard patent (R) registers a patent granted by a designated foreign patent office. A standard patent (O) is granted after substantive examination by the Registrar. A short-term patent is granted after formality examination only. Standard patents run for 20 years from filing (s.39(1)(b)); short-term patents run for 8 years from filing (s.126(1)(b)). [cites: Cap. 514 2; Cap. 514 3; Cap. 514 8; Cap. 514 37T; Cap. 514 37U; Cap. 514 37X; Cap. 514 117; Cap. 514 118; Cap. 514 39; Cap. 514 126; s.3(a)-(b) (standard patent (R) application = recording and registering a designated patent application); s.8 (designation of patent offices by Chief Executive in Council notice); s.37T(1), s.37U(2)-(3), s.37X(1) (standard patent (O) substantive examination and grant); s.117 (short-term formality examination only); s.118(1) (grant); s.39(1)(b) and s.126(1)(b).]
- [V] **map-4** Designs are registered under the Registered Designs Ordinance (Cap. 522). A design must be new (s.5) and must be features of shape, configuration, pattern or ornament applied to an article and judged by the eye (s.2). The initial registration period is 5 years from filing, extendable in 5-year steps to a total of 25 years (s.28). Computer programs are not registrable (s.8(1)). [cites: Cap. 522 2; Cap. 522 5; Cap. 522 8; Cap. 522 28; s.2(1) (definition of design); s.5(1)-(2) (new designs registrable); s.8(1) (computer programs not registrable); s.28(1)-(2) (initial 5 years; total not over 25 years from filing).]
- [R] **map-5** Trade marks are registered under Cap. 559, and a registered trade mark is a property right (s.10(1)). Unregistered marks are protected only by passing off, because the Ordinance gives no infringement proceedings for an unregistered mark but keeps the law of passing off (s.10(3)). [cites: Cap. 559 10; Cap. 559 3; s.10(1) (registered mark is property right); s.10(3) (no proceedings for infringement of unregistered mark; passing off preserved).] To check: Per the brief, the (R) point stays R. Held text confirms the registered-mark property right (s.10(1)) and that s.10(3) gives no statutory infringement action for an unregistered mark while preserving passing off. What still needs checking: the elements of passing off (goodwill, misrepresentation, damage) and whether any other route protects unregistered marks, which requires case law (for example the HK Court of Final Appeal and CFI passing-off authorities) not in the held texts.
- [R] **map-6** Hong Kong has no trade secrets statute. Confidential information is protected by the equitable action for breach of confidence and by contract. To check: Not checkable from held text. The absence of a trade secrets statute needs a check of the HK e-Legislation index, which was not held. The breach of confidence action rests on case law (for example Coco v A N Clark, which the trade-secrets stage cites) and is not in the held texts. Computer-misuse and data-protection provisions held in Caps. 200, 106 and 486 are not trade secret rights, so they do not contradict the point.
- [V] **map-7** Moral rights exist under ss.89-98: a right to be identified as author and a right to object to derogatory treatment. Neither applies to a computer program or a computer-generated work. The identification right is excluded by s.91(2)(a) and (c), and the integrity right by s.93(2). [cites: Cap. 528 89; Cap. 528 91; Cap. 528 92; Cap. 528 93; Cap. 528 98; s.89(1) (right to be identified); s.91(2)(a) (computer program) and (c) (computer-generated work); s.92(1)-(3) (integrity right); s.93(2) (integrity right does not apply to a computer program or computer-generated work).]
- [V] **map-8** Some copyright and trade mark conduct is criminal. Copyright offences cover dealing in and possessing infringing copies for trade or business (s.118, including the business possession offence for programs in s.118(2A)). Trade mark offences in Cap. 362 cover forging and falsely applying marks (s.9), which is narrower than general infringement. Investigation sits with the Commissioner of Customs and Excise and the authorized officers appointed by the Commissioner, who have entry, search and seizure powers under s.122 (copyright) and s.14 (trade descriptions). [cites: Cap. 528 118; Cap. 528 122; Cap. 528 198; Cap. 362 2; Cap. 362 9; Cap. 362 14; 528 s.118(1)(a)-(g) and s.118(2A); s.122(1)-(2) (powers of authorized officers); s.198 (definitions of authorized officer and Commissioner). 362 s.9(1)(a)-(b) (forgery and false application); s.2 (Commissioner means the Commissioner of Customs and Excise); s.14(1) (appointment of authorized officers).]
- [V] **map-a1** The only register under the Copyright Ordinance is the register of copyright licensing bodies, kept by the Registrar of Copyright Licensing Bodies. Nothing in the Ordinance registers copyright works, so a bank cannot register its software for copyright protection. [cites: Cap. 528 146; Cap. 528 147; s.147(1) (register of copyright licensing bodies); s.146 (Registrar of Copyright Licensing Bodies); absence from the held section list of any work registration provision.]
- [V] **map-a2** Computer programs cannot be registered as designs under the Registered Designs Ordinance. The exclusion is for the program itself; whether a screen or icon is registrable as a design is a separate question (see product-7). [cites: Cap. 522 8; Cap. 522 2; s.8(1) (computer programs and protected layout-designs not registrable); s.8(2) (rules may exclude articles of a primarily literary or artistic character); s.2(1) (design definition).]
- [V] **map-a3** Criminal prosecutions under the Copyright Ordinance must be started within 3 years from the date the offence was committed. This limit makes the copyright criminal route a short-term exposure compared with civil claims. [cites: Cap. 528 120A; s.120A (time limit for prosecutions; 3 years from commission).]

## copyright
- [V] **copyright-1** A computer program is a literary work. Preparatory design material for a computer program is also a literary work, and so is a compilation of data that is original by selection or arrangement. [cites: Cap. 528 4; s.4(1)(a) (compilations), (b) (computer program), (c) (preparatory design material for a computer program).]
- [V] **copyright-2** Copyright subsists only in original works, and a literary work (including a program) is protected only once it is recorded, in writing or otherwise. [cites: Cap. 528 2; Cap. 528 4; s.2(1)(a) (original literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works); s.2(3) (qualification requirements); s.4(2)-(3) (recording).]
- [V] **copyright-3** Copyright in a literary work lasts for the author's life plus 50 years, counted from the end of the calendar year of death. For a computer-generated work, copyright lasts 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was made. [cites: Cap. 528 17; s.17(2) (life plus 50); s.17(3)-(4) (unknown authorship); s.17(6) (computer-generated works); s.17(7) (joint authorship).]
- [V] **copyright-4** The owner's exclusive acts include copying, issuing copies, renting, public performance, communication to the public and adaptation (s.22(1)). Copying includes reproduction in any material form, storage by electronic means, and transient or incidental copies (s.23(2), (6)). [cites: Cap. 528 22; Cap. 528 23; s.22(1)(a)-(g) (restricted acts, paragraphs (d) and (f) blank in held text); s.22(2) (infringement by doing or authorizing); s.23(2) (copying includes electronic storage); s.23(6) (transient or incidental copies).]
- [V] **copyright-5** For a computer program, adaptation means an arrangement or altered version of the program or a translation of it. A translation includes converting the program into or out of a computer language or code, or into a different computer language or code. [cites: Cap. 528 29; s.29(1) (adaptation made when recorded); s.29(2) (acts in relation to an adaptation); s.29(3)(b) (adaptation of a program); s.29(4) (translation includes conversion into, out of or between computer languages or codes).]
- [V] **copyright-6** Communicating a work to the public is a restricted act. The right is framed by electronic communication, covering broadcasting, inclusion in a cable service and making available online so that the public can access the work from a place and time they choose. [cites: Cap. 528 22; Cap. 528 28A; s.22(1)(fa) (restricted act: communication to the public); s.28A(1) (act restricted); s.28A(2) (electronic communication, including making available); s.28A(3) (making available by wire or wireless means); s.28A(4)-(6) (who communicates; mere facilities).]
- [V] **copyright-7** Secondary infringement (importing or exporting, dealing with, and the other secondary acts) requires that the defendant knows or has reason to believe the copy is an infringing copy. Primary infringement under s.22 does not need that knowledge. [cites: Cap. 528 30; Cap. 528 31; s.30 (importing or exporting, other than for private and domestic use); s.31(1)(a)-(d) (possessing, selling, exhibiting, distributing); s.31(2)-(3) (trade and prejudice factors).]
- [V] **copyright-8** A lawful user is a person with a contractual right to use the program (s.60(2)). A lawful user may make a back-up copy where that is necessary for the lawful use (s.60(1)), and may copy or adapt where necessary for lawful use, including to correct errors (s.61(1)-(2)). A contract can exclude the back-up right (s.60(3)). The held text has no express contractual override for s.61. [cites: Cap. 528 60; Cap. 528 61; s.60(1) (back-up copy); s.60(2) (lawful user = contractual right to use); s.60(3) (subject to any agreement to the contrary); s.61(1) (copy or adapt if necessary for lawful use); s.61(2) (error correction); s.61(3) (no overlap with s.60).]
- [V] **copyright-9** Fair dealing is available only for the purposes the Ordinance lists, with no general fair use. These include research and private study (s.38), criticism, review, quotation and reporting current events (s.39), and parody, satire, caricature and pastiche (s.39A). The Ordinance also lists fair dealing for instruction (s.41A) and public administration (s.54A). Courts weigh purpose and nature, the work, the amount taken and the market effect (ss.38(3), 39(4)), and criticism, review and quotation need a sufficient acknowledgement unless impracticable (s.39(1)(b), (6)). [cites: Cap. 528 37; Cap. 528 38; Cap. 528 39; Cap. 528 39A; Cap. 528 41A; Cap. 528 54A; s.37(3) (primary consideration: no conflict with normal exploitation and no unreasonable prejudice); s.37(5) (provisions construed independently); s.38(1) and (3); s.39(1)(b), (2)(d), (3), (4), (6); s.39A(1)-(2); s.41A and s.54A (fair dealing headings).]
- [V] **copyright-10** Business end-user offence: it is a criminal offence to possess, without licence, an infringing copy of a computer program for the purpose of or in the course of a trade or business, with a view to its being used in that business (s.118(2A)-(2B)). It does not cover printed-form copies (s.118(2C)) or programs that are technically required to view or listen to a work (s.118(2D)). On indictment the penalty is a level 5 fine per infringing copy and 4 years' imprisonment (s.119(1)). Directors, managers and similar officers commit the offence too if it is committed with their consent or connivance, or is attributable to them (s.125(1)). [cites: Cap. 528 118; Cap. 528 119; Cap. 528 125; s.118(2A) (offence); s.118(2B)(a) (computer program); s.118(2C) (printed form excluded); s.118(2D)(a)-(b) (technically required programs excluded); s.119(1) (penalty on indictment); s.125(1) (body corporate and officers), s.125(2)-(3) (members and partners).] (Supervisor: see using-1 for the defences in s.118(3)-(3B), which are held.)
- [V] **copyright-11** The owner may sue for infringement and obtain the usual property remedies, including damages, injunctions and accounts (s.107). The court may award additional damages having regard to flagrancy, benefit to the defendant, conduct and wide circulation (s.108(2)). Damages are barred only where the defendant did not know, and had no reason to believe, that copyright subsisted in the work; other remedies remain available (s.108(1)). [cites: Cap. 528 107; Cap. 528 108; s.107(1)-(2) (actionable by owner; damages, injunctions, accounts); s.108(1) (innocent infringer: no damages, without prejudice to other remedies); s.108(2)(a)-(e) (additional damages factors).]
- [V] **copyright-12** Effective technological measures (access control or copy control) are protected. Circumventing them with knowledge or reason to believe is actionable (s.273A), making or dealing in circumvention devices is actionable (s.273B) and some commercial dealings are offences (s.273C), subject to exceptions (ss.273D-273H; the Secretary may exclude works or devices by gazette notice under s.273H). Interference with rights management information is actionable under s.274, but only where the defendant knows or has reason to believe the act induces, enables, facilitates or conceals infringement (s.274(2A)). [cites: Cap. 528 273; Cap. 528 273A; Cap. 528 273B; Cap. 528 273C; Cap. 528 273H; Cap. 528 274; s.273(1)-(3) (circumvent; effective technological measure; technological measure); s.273A(1) (knowledge or reason to believe); s.273C(1)(a)-(g) and (2) (circumvention devices and services); s.273H (gazetted exclusions); s.274(2), (2A) (rights management information).]
- [V] **copyright-13** In civil proceedings, a name that purports to be the author's on copies of the work as published, or on the work when made, is presumed to be the author and to have made the work outside employment (s.115(2)). If the author is dead or cannot be identified after reasonable inquiry, the work is presumed to be original (s.115(5)). [cites: Cap. 528 115; s.115(2)(a)-(b) (author presumption; made outside s.14(1) employee works); s.115(3) (joint authorship); s.115(4) (publisher presumption); s.115(5)(a) (original work presumption if author dead or unknown).]
- [V] **copyright-14** Section 187 is the only groundless-threats provision in the Copyright Ordinance, and it is narrow. It covers only threats of proceedings under ss.30-31 over copies of a work that are infringing only because they were imported (s.35(3)), where the copy was lawfully made in the place where it was made. It does not cover threats about software generally, and a mere notice of copyright is not a threat (s.187(3)). [cites: Cap. 528 187; Cap. 528 35; s.187(1)(a)-(c) (reliefs); s.187(2) (defence: the acts would have been infringing); s.187(3) (notification is not a threat); s.187(5) (counterclaim); s.35(3) (imported copies infringing).]
- [R] **copyright-15** The Copyright Ordinance has no statutory rule on idea and expression. Hong Kong courts are reported to treat English authorities (such as Navitaire v easyJet and SAS Institute v World Programming) as persuasive on what is protected in software. [cites: Cap. 528 2; Cap. 528 4; s.4(1)(a) (compilation protected by selection or arrangement as an intellectual creation); s.2(1)(a) (original works).] To check: Per the brief, this stays R. Held text confirms the Ordinance has no idea/expression section and protects selection and arrangement under s.4(1)(a). To check: the HK authorities that adopt or reject Navitaire v easyJet and SAS Institute v World Programming (citations and holdings), and whether a CFI or CFA decision treats them as persuasive. Those cases are not in the held texts and were not verified.
- [V] **copyright-a1** The Copyright Ordinance has no express decompilation or interoperability exception. Reverse engineering a program to make it interoperate is permitted only if it falls within the lawful-user rule, that is, it is necessary for the lawful use of a copy the user is licensed to use (s.61(1)), or for error correction (s.61(2)). [cites: Cap. 528 60; Cap. 528 61; s.61(1) (copy or adapt where necessary for lawful use); s.61(2) (error correction); s.61(3) (no overlap with s.60). Absence: no heading in the full held section list (ss.1-283) refers to decompilation or interoperability.]
- [V] **copyright-a2** A database or table is protected as a literary work if its selection or arrangement of data is an intellectual creation, even if the data itself is not original. The held Ordinance has no separate database right. [cites: Cap. 528 4; s.4(1)(a) (compilation of data or other material, including a table, protected by selection or arrangement); absence of a separate database right in the held section list.]
- [V] **copyright-a3** For the offence in s.118(1), a lawful user counts as one who has a contractual right to use the program in any place in or outside Hong Kong. This extends the lawful-user rule to cross-border licences, but s.118A refers only to offences under s.118(1), not to the business possession offence in s.118(2A). [cites: Cap. 528 118A; Cap. 528 118; s.118A(a) (contractual right to use in any place in or outside Hong Kong for ss.60-61 purposes in proceedings for s.118(1) offences); s.118(1) and (2A) (offences).]
- [V] **copyright-a4** A copy of a computer program that was lawfully made in the country where it was made is not an infringing copy for the purpose of the importation rule in s.35(3). This protects a bank that imports licensed copies made abroad, but only on the s.35(3) limb; the breach-of-exclusive-licence limb in s.35(3)(b) is still relevant. [cites: Cap. 528 35; Cap. 528 35A; s.35A(1)-(2)(a) (copy of computer program lawfully made abroad is not an infringing copy for s.35(3)); s.35(3)(a)-(b) (importation limb and breach of exclusive licence); s.35(4) (separate test for the criminal provisions in ss.118-133).]
- [V] **copyright-a5** Authorising infringement is a separate basis of liability. In deciding whether a person authorised another's restricted act, the court may consider the extent of the person's power to control or prevent infringement, the relationship between them, and whether the person took reasonable steps to limit or stop it. [cites: Cap. 528 22; s.22(2) (infringement by doing or authorizing); s.22(2A)(a)-(c) (control, relationship, reasonable steps).]

## ownership
- [V] **ownership-1** The author of a work is its first copyright owner (s.13), and the author is the person who creates it (s.11(1)). For a computer-generated literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work, the author is the person who undertook the arrangements necessary for its creation (s.11(3)). Section 13 yields to ss.14 and 15, and s.16 disapplies ss.13 to 15 for Government, Legislative Council and certain international organisation copyright. [cites: Cap. 528 11; Cap. 528 13; Cap. 528 16; s.11(1) and (3); s.13 (subject to ss.14-16); s.16]
- [V] **ownership-2** Where an employee makes a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work, or a film, in the course of employment, the employer is first owner of the copyright, unless an agreement says otherwise. If the work is then exploited in a way neither side could reasonably have contemplated when it was made, the employer must pay the employee an award, unless the parties agreed otherwise. [cites: Cap. 528 14; s.14(1)(a) and (b); s.14(2)]
- [V] **ownership-3** A commissioned work's copyright belongs to whoever the commissioning agreement expressly names (s.15(1)). Whatever the agreement says, the commissioner has an exclusive licence to exploit the work for all purposes reasonably contemplated when it was commissioned, and may restrain exploitation for purposes it could reasonably object to (s.15(2)). Without an express term, the author stays first owner under s.13, subject to that licence. [cites: Cap. 528 15; Cap. 528 13; Cap. 528 103; s.15(1); s.15(2)(a) and (b) (notwithstanding s.15(1), ss.13 and 103)]
- [V] **ownership-4** A joint work is one made by two or more authors collaborating where each contribution is not distinct from the others'. Where the Ordinance refers to 'the author' of a joint work, that means all the joint authors (s.12(4)). A film is treated as jointly authored unless its producer and principal director are the same person (s.12(2)). [cites: Cap. 528 12; s.12(1), (2) and (4)]
- [V] **ownership-5** An assignment of copyright is effective only if it is in writing signed by or for the assignor (s.101(3)). A licence binds successors in title except a good-faith purchaser for value without notice (s.101(4)). An exclusive licence must be in writing signed by the copyright owner (s.103(1)). An exclusive licensee has, against everyone except the owner, the rights and remedies of an assignee from the grant, subject to the defences the owner would face (s.112(1) and (3)). Prospective ownership of future copyright is dealt with in s.102. [cites: Cap. 528 101; Cap. 528 102; Cap. 528 103; Cap. 528 112; s.101(3) and (4); s.102(1) and (3); s.103(1); s.112(1) and (3)]
- [V] **ownership-6** Moral rights cannot be assigned (s.105) but can be waived by an instrument in writing signed by the person giving up the right (s.98(2)). A waiver may cover specific works, a class of works, or future works, may be conditional or revocable, and if given in favour of the owner it extends to licensees and successors unless a contrary intention is stated (s.98(3)). [cites: Cap. 528 98; Cap. 528 105; s.98(2) and (3)(a) and (b); s.105]
- [V] **ownership-7** An electronic signature can satisfy a signature requirement, such as the one for a copyright assignment under s.101(3), but only if the method identifies the signer and shows approval, is reliable and appropriate for the purpose, and the other party consents to that method (553 s.6(1)). A bare online click is not automatically an electronic signature. The matters in Schedule 1 (for example land dispositions and wills) are excluded, but copyright and patent assignments are not listed there. [cites: Cap. 553 5; Cap. 553 6; Cap. 553 3; Cap. 553 15; Cap. 553 17; Cap. 553 Sch. 1; 553 s.5(1); s.6(1)(b)-(e); s.3 and Sch. 1; s.15(1) and (2A) (consent rules cover ss.5, 5A and 7); s.17(2A)]
- [V] **ownership-8** The inventor owns the right to a patent (s.9E(1)), but that is subject to the law of the place of employment for an employee (s.9E(2)) and to the earlier-filing rule for independent inventors (s.9E(3)). As between employer and employee, an invention belongs to the employer if made in the course of normal or specifically assigned duties where an invention might reasonably be expected, or where the employee had a special obligation to further the employer's interests (s.57(1)); otherwise it belongs to the employee (s.57(2)). The employee can claim compensation for outstanding benefit (s.58(1)), and a contract term diminishing the employee's rights is unenforceable against the employee (s.60(2)). [cites: Cap. 514 9E; Cap. 514 57; Cap. 514 58; Cap. 514 59; Cap. 514 60; s.9E(1), (2), (3); s.57(1)(a) and (b), (2); s.58(1); s.60(2)]
- [V] **ownership-9** A non-party can enforce a contract term if the contract expressly allows it or the term purports to confer a benefit on it, provided the party is identified in the contract by name, as a class member, or by description, so a named group company qualifies (s.4(1) and (2)). The term is not enforceable by the third party if the proper construction shows it was not intended to be (s.4(3)). The Ordinance does not give a third party a right to enforce a contract of employment against an employee (s.3(4)), and applies only to contracts made on or after commencement (s.3(1)). [cites: Cap. 623 3; Cap. 623 4; s.3(1) and (4); s.4(1)(a) and (b), (2), (3)]
- [V] **ownership-a1** An employee whose work is exploited in a way neither side could reasonably have contemplated when it was made is entitled to an award from the employer, at an agreed amount or as fixed by the Copyright Tribunal, unless the parties agreed otherwise (s.14(2)). [cites: Cap. 528 14; s.14(2)]
- [V] **ownership-a2** An employee who assigned an invention, or granted an exclusive licence under it, to the employer can apply to court for compensation if the patent is of outstanding benefit or if the benefit the employee received is inadequate compared with the employer's benefit (s.58(1) and (2)). The compensation right applies despite any term of the contract (s.58(3)), and the amount is a fair share of the employer's benefit (s.59(1)). [cites: Cap. 514 58; Cap. 514 59; s.58(1)(a)-(c) and (2)(a)-(d); s.58(3); s.59(1)]
- [V] **ownership-a3** A transfer of a patent or application can lose out to a later transaction: a later acquirer who took without knowledge of the earlier transaction is preferred if the earlier one had not been registered (or, for an unpublished standard patent or a short-term patent, notified to the Registrar) at the later date (s.52(1)). A new proprietor or exclusive licensee who does not register within 6 months of the transaction cannot recover damages or an account of profits for infringements between the transaction and registration (s.87). [cites: Cap. 514 52; Cap. 514 87; s.52(1)(a)(i)-(ii) and (b); s.52(3)(a)-(e); s.87(a) and (b)]
- [V] **ownership-a4** For a registered design, the designer is the original owner, unless the design is created for money or money's worth under a commission, in which case the commissioner is the original owner, subject to a contrary agreement (s.3(1) and (2)). A design made by an employee in the course of employment is owned by the employer, subject to a contrary agreement (s.3(3)). This reverses the copyright default in ss.13 and 15 for commissioned designs. [cites: Cap. 522 3; s.3(1), (2), (3), (4), (5)]
- [V] **ownership-a5** Where two or more persons invent the same thing independently, the right to a patent belongs to the person whose application has the earlier or earliest material date (s.9E(3)). That rule does not apply to a standard patent application that has not been published under s.20 or s.37Q (s.9E(4)). [cites: Cap. 514 9E; s.9E(3) and (4)]

## patents
- [V] **patents-1** An invention is patentable only if it is new, meaning it does not form part of the state of the art (s.9B); involves an inventive step, meaning it is not obvious to a person skilled in the art (s.9C); is susceptible of industrial application, meaning it can be made or used in any kind of industry (s.9D); and is not excluded by s.9A(2) to (6). [cites: Cap. 514 9A; Cap. 514 9B; Cap. 514 9C; Cap. 514 9D; s.9A(1)(a)-(c); s.9B(1) and (2); s.9C(1); s.9D]
- [V] **patents-2** Discoveries, scientific theories and mathematical methods, aesthetic creations, schemes or methods for mental acts, games or doing business, programs for a computer, and presentations of information are not inventions, but only to the extent the patent or application relates to them as such (s.9A(2)(a)-(d) and (3)). [cites: Cap. 514 9A; s.9A(2)(a), (b), (c), (d); s.9A(3)]
- [R] **patents-3** The Hong Kong courts and the Registrar are reported to follow the English approach to the 'as such' exclusion for computer programs and business methods. The held text does not say which test applies. [cites: Cap. 514 9A; s.9A(3)] To check: Not checkable from held text. - [V] **permissive-a1** For a program, an adaptation includes an arrangement, an altered version or a translation, and a translation includes conversion into a different computer language or code. So a modified or ported version is an adaptation whose making is restricted unless the licence or the lawful-user rules permit it. [cites: Cap. 528 29; s.29(3)(b) and (4); s.29(1)-(2)]
- [V] **permissive-a2** Secondary infringement by possession or distribution in trade or business needs knowledge or reason to believe that the copy is infringing (s.31(1)(a)-(c), (2)). A bank that receives third-party code in breach of its licence is therefore not caught by s.31 unless it has that knowledge or reason to believe. [cites: Cap. 528 31; s.31(1)(a)-(c) and (2)]

## copyleft
- [V] **copyleft-1** GPL-3.0 s.0 defines propagate and convey by reference to what applicable copyright law makes infringement. In Hong Kong the restricted acts in ss.22-29 and the secondary infringement sections ss.30-34 decide whether an act is propagation. [cites: GPL-3.0-only 0; Cap. 528 22; Cap. 528 30; Cap. 528 31; Cap. 528 32; Cap. 528 33; Cap. 528 34; GPL s.0 ('propagate' and 'convey'); 528 s.22(1)-(2); ss.30-34]
- [V] **copyleft-2** Whether a program that links to another is a work based on it turns first on the GPL definition of modify (GPL-3.0 s.0). Under Hong Kong copyright it turns on whether a substantial part is copied (s.22(3)) or an adaptation is made (s.29(3)(b)). No Hong Kong ruling on linking was found in the held sources (R). [cites: GPL-3.0-only 0; Cap. 528 22; Cap. 528 29; GPL s.0 ('modify' and 'based on'); 528 s.22(3)(a); 528 s.29(3)(b)]
- [V] **copyleft-3** GPL-3.0 s.3 is a waiver by each conveyor of its own power to forbid circumvention, limited to circumvention done by exercising licence rights. It does not waive other copyright owners' rights, and Hong Kong anti-circumvention liability under s.273A still applies to circumvention outside the licence, subject to the s.273D exceptions. [cites: GPL-3.0-only 3; Cap. 528 273A; Cap. 528 273D; GPL s.3 (both paragraphs); 528 s.273A(1)-(2); 528 s.273D(1)(c)]
- [V] **copyleft-4** The held Ordinance has no express decompilation or reverse-engineering exception to infringement. Section 61 lets a lawful user copy or adapt where necessary for lawful use, including error correction, and s.273D(1)(c) excuses circumvention only for interoperability. Reverse engineering for debugging is therefore a licence permission, as in LGPL-2.1 s.6 and LGPL-3.0 s.4, not a statutory right. [cites: LGPL-2.1-only 6; LGPL-3.0-only 4; Cap. 528 61; Cap. 528 273D; LGPL-2.1 s.6; LGPL-3.0 s.4(d); 528 s.61(1)-(2); 528 s.273D(1)(b)-(c)]
- [V] **copyleft-5** Under GPL-3.0 s.8, any propagation or modification not permitted by the licence automatically terminates the licensee's rights. Rights from a copyright holder are reinstated provisionally when the violation stops, permanently if the holder does not give notice within 60 days, or on a first notice if the violation is cured within 30 days. [cites: GPL-3.0-only 8; s.8 paragraphs 1 to 3]
- [V] **copyleft-a1** Circumventing a technological measure on a program for the sole purpose of interoperability with an independently created program is excused from s.273A only if the copy is not infringing and the identification or analysis is not itself an infringement (s.273D(1)(b)-(e)). The exception excuses circumvention, not copying or adapting. [cites: Cap. 528 273D; s.273D(1)(b), (c), (d) and (e)]
- [V] **copyleft-a2** Circumventing an effective technological measure on a copyright work gives the owner or an exclusive licensee civil remedies under s.273A only where the person knows or has reason to believe the act circumvents the measure, and subject to the s.273D and s.273H exceptions. [cites: Cap. 528 273A; Cap. 528 273H; s.273A(1)-(2)]

## network
- [V] **network-1** AGPL-3.0 s.13 is triggered only where the licensee modifies the Program and the modified version supports remote interaction over a network, and then requires offering all such users the Corresponding Source. This is a licence trigger, distinct from the Hong Kong communication right in s.28A, which turns on making a work available to the public and on who determines its content. [cites: AGPL-3.0-only 13; Cap. 528 28A; AGPL s.13 (first paragraph); 528 s.28A(1), (2)(c), (3), (4)-(6)]
- [R] **network-2** Source-available licences such as SSPL and BUSL are not among the held licence texts, and no Hong Kong decision enforcing them was located. Their enforcement by contract and copyright cannot be checked from held text. [cites: Cap. 528 22; s.22(1)(a), (fa) (copyright framework only)] To check: Not checkable from held text. Needs the SSPL and BUSL licence texts and a Hong Kong case search, with a case citation and holding.
- [V] **network-a1** A host communicates a work to the public in Hong Kong when it has determined the content of the communication (s.28A(4)). Clicking a link or merely providing facilities does not do so (s.28A(5)-(6)), but facilities primarily designed for direct access to a work without permission are not mere facilities. [cites: Cap. 528 28A; s.28A(4), (5), (6) and the note to s.28A(6)]
- [V] **network-a2** Making a work available online so that members of the public can access it from a place and at a time they choose is a communication to the public and so a restricted act, whether or not anyone downloads a copy. [cites: Cap. 528 28A; Cap. 528 22; s.28A(2)(c) and (3); s.22(1)(fa)]
- [V] **network-a3** Under s.29(2), doing the acts in s.28A (including making available) in relation to an adaptation is also restricted. So communicating a modified version of a program over a network is restricted under Hong Kong copyright, separately from the AGPL s.13 licence trigger. [cites: Cap. 528 29; Cap. 528 28A; s.29(2) with s.28A(1)]

## using
- [V] **using-1** Possessing an infringing copy of a computer program, without the owner's licence, for the purpose of or in the course of a trade or business with a view to its use in that business, is an offence (s.118(2A)-(2B)). Printed copies and programs technically required to view or hear a work are outside it (s.118(2C)-(2D)). It is a defence for the accused to prove they did not know and had no reason to believe the copy was infringing (s.118(3)). An employee who was given the copy by the employer for use at work has a defence (s.118(3A)), but not one who could make or influence the decision to acquire, use or remove it (s.118(3B)). On indictment: a level 5 fine per infringing copy and 4 years' imprisonment (s.119(1)). A director, manager or similar officer also commits the offence if the company's offence was committed with their consent or connivance or is attributable to them (s.125(1)). [cites: Cap. 528 118; Cap. 528 119; Cap. 528 125; s.118(2A) to (2D); s.119(1); s.125(1) and (2). The held text of s.118 ends at (2F)(b), so the defences the outline mentions in s.118(3) to (8) cannot be checked here.]
- [V] **using-2** The back-up and copy-or-adapt rights for a lawful user (ss.60 and 61) make the relevant act non-infringing in proceedings for an offence under s.118(1), not for the business end-user offence in s.118(2A). For that purpose a lawful user is anyone with a contractual right to use the program, in or outside Hong Kong. [cites: Cap. 528 118A; Cap. 528 60; Cap. 528 61; s.118A(a) (and (b) for works other than programs under s.35A(1)); s.60(2) and (3); s.61(1) to (3).]
- [V] **using-3** A designated critical infrastructure operator must keep a computer-system security management unit (s.21). It must submit and implement a security management plan covering Schedule 3 within 3 months of designation (s.23). It must carry out a computer-system security risk assessment at least every 12 months (s.24) and an audit at least every 24 months (s.25). It must notify computer-system security incidents to the Commissioner within 12 hours if the core function is disrupted or likely to be, otherwise within 48 hours, with a written report within 14 days (s.28; Sch. 6). For authorized institutions the designated authority is the Monetary Authority (the HKMA) (Sch. 2, Part 2, item 1). [cites: Cap. 653 21; Cap. 653 23; Cap. 653 24; Cap. 653 25; Cap. 653 28; Cap. 653 Sch. 2; Cap. 653 Sch. 3; Cap. 653 Sch. 6; s.21(1) and (4); s.23(1)(a), (3), (5); s.24(1)(a)(i) and (ii); s.25(1)(a)(i) and (ii); s.28(2), (4); Sch. 6 items 1 and 3; Sch. 2 Pt 2 item 1 (Monetary Authority; regulated organizations include an authorized institution).]
- [V] **using-4** The HKMA's technology-risk expectations for a digital bank are guidance, not statute: TM-G-1 (technology risk, V.1, 2003), SA-2 (outsourcing, V.1, 2001) and OR-2 (operational resilience, 31 May 2022). The banking volume says the OR-2 deadline of 31 May 2026 has passed, and that the HKMA's June 2026 letter moved the focus to sustaining resilience. (Source: banking volume (fact-checked), stage technology. Guidance, not law.)
- [V] **using-5** A bank that engages a data processor, in or outside Hong Kong, must adopt contractual or other means to prevent unauthorised or accidental access, processing, erasure, loss or use of the personal data it transfers (DPP 4(2), cite cap 486 section 'Sch. 1 s.4'), and to stop the processor keeping it longer than necessary (DPP 2(3), section 'Sch. 1 s.2'). A data processor processes personal data on behalf of another and not for its own purposes (DPP 2(4)). [cites: Cap. 486 Sch. 1; Sch. 1 DPP 2(2) and (3); DPP 4(1) (held text ends at (1)(b)); DPP 4(2) not in held text.]
- [V] **using-a1** The business end-user offence covers computer programs, but not an infringing copy of a program held in printed form (s.118(2B)(a) and (2C)). The practical risk is unlicensed installed copies used in the business, not printed material. [cites: Cap. 528 118; s.118(2A), (2B)(a), (2C).]
- [V] **using-a2** Online acceptance by click: an offer and its acceptance may be expressed by electronic records, and a contract is not invalid only because electronic records were used (s.17(1)-(2)); the offeror may prescribe the method of acceptance (s.17(3)). Acceptance by conduct alone is a common-law question, not s.17. [cites: Cap. 553 17; s.17(1) and (3).]
- [V] **using-a3** A licence can cut back the back-up right in s.60 by agreement, because s.60(3) makes it subject to any agreement to the contrary. Section 61 (copying or adapting for lawful use, including correcting errors) has no equivalent words in the held text, so whether a licence can exclude s.61 is not settled by the held text. [cites: Cap. 528 60; Cap. 528 61; s.60(3); s.61(1) to (3).]
- [V] **using-a4** If the bank is designated as a critical infrastructure operator, a material change to the design, configuration, security or operation of a critical computer system must be notified to the regulator within 1 month (s.22(1)). A change is material if it affects computer-system security or the operator's ability to respond to threats, or makes earlier information provided under s.16 inaccurate in a material particular (s.22(3)). [cites: Cap. 653 22; s.22(1), (2)(a), (3)(a) and (b), (4), (5).]

## releasing
- [V] **releasing-1** Code written by an employee in the course of employment belongs to the employer, subject to any contrary agreement and to an award to the employee for exploitation that could not reasonably have been contemplated (s.14(1) and (2)). Code commissioned from a contractor belongs to the contractor unless the agreement expressly says otherwise; the commissioning bank then has only an exclusive licence for purposes reasonably contemplated when the work was commissioned, plus a power to restrain objectionable exploitation (s.15). A bank therefore owns contractor code only if its contract says so. [cites: Cap. 528 4; Cap. 528 14; Cap. 528 15; s.4 definition of literary work (includes a computer program); s.14(1) and (2); s.15(1) and (2)(a) and (b).]
- [V] **releasing-2** The integrity right against derogatory treatment (s.92) does not apply to a computer program, so the author of a program cannot rely on it when the code is changed or released under another licence. [cites: Cap. 528 92; Cap. 528 93; s.93(2); s.92(1) (the right belongs to the author of a literary work).]
- [V] **releasing-3** Publishing code that embodies an invention before the filing date makes it part of the state of the art (s.9B). The only relief is the six-month non-prejudicial disclosure rule for an evident abuse or a display at a recognised exhibition (s.11A for standard (R), s.37B for standard (O), s.109 for short-term). [cites: Cap. 514 9B; Cap. 514 11A; s.9B(1) and (2); s.11A(1) to (3).]
- [V] **releasing-4** Personal data should not go into released code or test data. Using personal data for a new purpose needs the data subject's prescribed consent (DPP 3(1), section 'Sch. 1 s.3'), and the bank must take all practicable steps against unauthorised access (DPP 4(1), section 'Sch. 1 s.4'). Applying these to source code and test data is a drafting inference. [cites: Cap. 486 Sch. 1; Sch. 1 DPP 3(1) and (4) (new purpose); DPP 4(1).]
- [V] **releasing-a1** An employee who wrote code may be owed an award if the employer, or someone with its permission, exploits it in a way that neither side could reasonably have contemplated when it was made. Absent agreement, the amount is agreed or set by the Copyright Tribunal, and the award applies subject to any agreement to the contrary. [cites: Cap. 528 14; s.14(2), read with s.14(1)(a).]
- [V] **releasing-a2** Where the bank commissioned code from a contractor, it can restrain any exploitation it could reasonably object to, even when it has only an exclusive licence (s.15(2)(b)). [cites: Cap. 528 15; s.15(2)(b); s.15(1) (the commissioner's rights apply notwithstanding ss.13 and 103).]

## deals
- [V] **deals-1** An assignment of copyright is ineffective unless it is in writing signed by or for the assignor (s.101(3)). A patent assignment or mortgage is void unless in writing and signed by or for the assignor (Patents s.50(6)). A trade mark assignment is not effective unless in writing and signed (s.27(4)); registration of the transaction is a separate step (s.29). [cites: Cap. 528 101; Cap. 514 50; Cap. 559 27; Cap. 559 29; 528 s.101(3); 514 s.50(2) and (6); 559 s.27(4); 559 s.29(1) to (4).]
- [V] **deals-2** An unregistered patent transaction can lose out to a later transaction if the later party took without knowledge of the earlier one and the earlier one was not registered (s.52(1)). A new owner or exclusive licensee who does not register within 6 months of the transaction cannot recover damages or an account of profits for infringements between the transaction and registration (s.87). [cites: Cap. 514 52; Cap. 514 87; s.52(1) to (3) (assignment, licence, mortgage and related events); s.87(a) and (b) (6-month period, and the court's discretion where registration was not practicable).]
- [V] **deals-3** The Cap. 71 controls on negligence, contract and reasonableness (ss.7 and 8) do not apply to a contract so far as it creates or transfers a right or interest in IP such as copyright or a patent (Sch. 1, para 1(c)). Whether an ordinary software licence creates an interest of that kind is not settled by the held text. Section 16 covers only contracts for the sale or passing of goods across borders, so it does not reach software licences. Section 17(1) means that if Hong Kong law applies only by the parties' choice, ss.7 to 12 do not operate as part of the proper law, unless the choice was made to evade Cap. 71 or a consumer habitually resident in Hong Kong is involved (s.17(2)). [cites: Cap. 71 Sch. 1; Cap. 71 2; Cap. 71 7; Cap. 71 8; Cap. 71 16; Cap. 71 17; Sch. 1 para 1(c) (ss.7, 8 and 9 do not apply); s.2(2) (ss.7 to 12 only for business liability); s.16(1) and (3) (exempted supply contracts are goods contracts); s.17(1) and (2)(a) and (b).]
- [V] **deals-4** In a contract to supply services in the course of a business, the implied terms are reasonable care and skill (s.5), performance within a reasonable time (s.6) and a reasonable charge (s.7) where the contract is silent. Where the customer deals as consumer, the supplier cannot exclude liability under the Ordinance by a contract term (s.8(1)). For other customers the terms can be varied by express agreement, subject to Cap. 71 (s.8(2)). [cites: Cap. 457 5; Cap. 457 6; Cap. 457 7; Cap. 457 8; s.5; s.6(1) and (2); s.7(1) and (2); s.8(1) (consumer only) and (2) (express agreement, subject to Cap. 71), with s.8(3).]
- [V] **deals-5** The court's unconscionability powers (refuse enforcement, enforce the rest, or revise the unconscionable part) apply only to contracts for the sale of goods or supply of services in which one party deals as consumer. They are judged by the circumstances at the time the contract was made. A business-to-business software licence would not generally fall within them. [cites: Cap. 458 5; Cap. 458 6; s.5(1) and (2); s.6(1)(a) to (e) and (2)(a), (3).]
- [V] **deals-6** In a winding up, the liquidator may, with leave of the court and within 12 months after the winding up starts, disclaim unprofitable contracts and other property that is not readily saleable because of onerous obligations (s.268(1)). A disclaimer ends the company's rights and liabilities in the property but does not affect other persons' rights except as needed to release the company (s.268(2)). The other party may apply to the court to rescind the contract and prove for damages (s.268(5)). The held text does not name licences or IP, and there is no held equivalent of US Bankruptcy Code s.365(n) (R). [cites: Cap. 32 268; s.268(1), (2), (4) (the 28-day application right and the deemed adoption of a contract) and (5).]
- [V] **deals-7** IP disputes, including those over ownership, validity, infringement, and transactions in an IP right, can be arbitrated (ss.103C and 103D(1)). An award is final and binding on the parties and on any person claiming through or under them, unless the parties agree otherwise (s.73(1)). A third-party licensee is not, merely because it holds a licence, treated as claiming through a party for that purpose (s.103E(2)), though its contract and operation-of-law rights against a party are preserved (s.103E(3)). [cites: Cap. 609 103C; Cap. 609 103D; Cap. 609 103E; Cap. 609 73; s.103C(a) and (b); s.103D(1) and (6); s.103E(2) to (4); s.73(1) and (2).]
- [V] **deals-8** Agreements between undertakings that prevent, restrict or distort competition in Hong Kong breach the first conduct rule (s.6), unless an exclusion applies. Exclusions include an agreement that improves production or promotes technical progress, passes a fair share of the benefit to consumers, is not more restrictive than needed and does not eliminate competition (Sch. 1, para 1), and lesser-significance agreements under a $200 million combined turnover threshold, which do not cover serious anti-competitive conduct (Sch. 1, para 5). An undertaking with substantial market power that abuses it breaches the second conduct rule (s.21). [cites: Cap. 619 6; Cap. 619 21; Cap. 619 Sch. 1; s.6(1) and (3); s.21(1) and (4); Sch. 1 para 1(a) to (c) and para 5(1) and (2).]
- [V] **deals-9** The HKMA's outsourcing guidance (SA-2, V.1, 2001) expects an authorized institution that plans to outsource a banking-related business area, back office included, or to change the scope of that outsourcing, to discuss the plan with the HKMA in advance. It should also satisfy the HKMA that the SA-2 section 2 issues are addressed before implementing (para 1.3.2). (Source: banking volume (fact-checked), stage technology, SA-2 card. Guidance, not law: 'should discuss'.)
- [V] **deals-a1** A licence granted by the copyright owner binds anyone who later acquires the copyright, unless the acquirer bought in good faith, for valuable consideration and without notice of the licence (s.101(4)). In an acquisition of code, licences already granted over it generally pass with it. [cites: Cap. 528 101; s.101(4).]
- [V] **deals-a2** An exclusive copyright licence gives the licensee the same rights and remedies, for matters after the grant, as an assignment would, but not against the copyright owner. The licensee's rights run concurrently with the owner's (s.112(1) and (2)). [cites: Cap. 528 112; s.112(1) to (3).]
- [V] **deals-a3** An assignment of a patent, or an exclusive patent licence, can transfer the right to sue for infringements that happened before the transfer (s.50(7)). Deal documents should say expressly whether that right passes. [cites: Cap. 514 50; s.50(7), with s.80 and s.88 referred to.]
- [V] **deals-a4** A new owner or licensee of a registered trade mark who does not register the transaction within 6 months cannot recover damages or an account of profits for infringements between the transaction and registration (s.29(4)). The bar does not apply if the court finds registration was not practicable within the 6 months and an application was made as soon as practicable. [cites: Cap. 559 29; s.29(3) (ineffective against a conflicting interest until registration) and s.29(4)(a) and (b).]
- [V] **deals-a5** A party may put the validity of a patent in issue in arbitration, despite Cap. 514 s.101(2) (s.103I). An arbitration clause can therefore cover patent validity disputes. [cites: Cap. 609 103I; s.103I.]

## ai
- [V] **ai-1** For a computer-generated literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work, the author is the person who undertook the arrangements necessary for its creation. Copyright in it lasts 50 years from the end of the year it was made, and the right to be identified as author does not apply to it. [cites: Cap. 528 11; Cap. 528 17; Cap. 528 198; Cap. 528 91; s.11(3) (author of computer-generated work); s.198(1) definition of computer-generated (no human author); s.17(6) (50 years from end of year made); s.91(2)(c) (no right to be identified).]
- [V] **ai-2** The held Copyright Ordinance has no text and data mining exception, so copying a work to train an AI model needs the owner's licence or another permitted act. Whether an AI copyright amendment has been enacted is not checkable from the held text. [cites: Cap. 528 23; Cap. 528 38; s.23(2) (storing a work electronically is copying); the section list in Cap. 528 has no text or data mining section; s.38 is the only research exception.]
- [V] **ai-3** Research or private study fair dealing (s.38) is narrow: it covers only that purpose, is judged by the s.38(3) factors including commercial nature and effect on the market, and does not protect copying that the copier knows will go to several people for the same purpose. Transient or incidental copies are not restricted under s.23(6), but the s.65A exemption for temporary copies applies only to service providers that meet its conditions. [cites: Cap. 528 23; Cap. 528 38; Cap. 528 65A; s.38(1) (fair dealing for research or private study); s.38(2)(b) (copies to more than one person); s.38(3) (factors); s.23(6) (transient or incidental copies); s.65A(1) (service provider conditions).]
- [V] **ai-4** Collecting personal data for training must be for a lawful purpose directly related to the bank's function, and the data subject must be told the purpose and classes of transferees on or before collection. Using the data for a new purpose needs the data subject's prescribed consent. The PCPD model framework for AI is guidance only and is not checked here. [cites: Cap. 486 Sch. 1; DPP 1(1)(a)-(c) and 1(3) (collection and notice); DPP 3(1) and (4) (use for a new purpose).]
- [V] **ai-5** The HKMA's August 2024 circular applies its consumer-protection principles (governance and accountability, fairness, transparency and disclosure, data privacy) to customer-facing generative AI, so chatbots, robo-advice and AI marketing need board accountability, fairness testing and disclosure before go-live. The HKMA-Cyberport GenA.I. Sandbox gives supervisory feedback but no legal exemption. (Source: banking volume (fact-checked), stage technology, GenAI circular card (19 August 2024).)
- [V] **ai-6** The right to a patent belongs to the inventor or the inventor's successor in title (s.9E(1)), and the inventor has a right to be mentioned as such (s.9F). The held text does not define 'inventor', so whether an AI system can be named as inventor is R and untested in Hong Kong. [cites: Cap. 514 9E; Cap. 514 9F; s.9E(1) (right belongs to inventor); s.9E(2)-(3) (employee and independent invention rules); s.9F(1) (right to be mentioned).]
- [V] **ai-a1** Loading a work into a training dataset or model store is itself copying, because storing a work in any medium by electronic means is copying, so the input stage needs a licence or a permitted act even if no output is published. [cites: Cap. 528 23; s.23(2) (storing by electronic means is copying).]
- [V] **ai-a2** Fair dealing for research or private study does not protect a person who copies for someone else, such as a team sharing one dataset, if the copier knows or has reason to believe the copies will go to more than one person at the same time for the same purpose. [cites: Cap. 528 38; s.38(2)(b) (copying by a person other than the researcher or student).]
- [V] **ai-a3** Personal data for training must be collected for a lawful purpose directly related to a function or activity of the bank, and the amount collected must be adequate but not excessive for that purpose (DPP 1(1)), so scraping or bulk collection beyond that purpose breaches the principle. [cites: Cap. 486 Sch. 1; DPP 1(1)(a)-(c) and 1(2) (lawful and fair collection).]
- [V] **ai-a4** A bank that gives processing or model-hosting work to a data processor, in Hong Kong or abroad, must use a contract or other means to stop that processor keeping the personal data longer than needed for the processing (DPP 2(3)). [cites: Cap. 486 Sch. 1; DPP 2(3) and 2(4) (data processor defined as processing on behalf of another and not for its own purposes).]
- [V] **ai-a5** If two or more persons make the same invention independently, the patent right goes to the one whose application has the earlier or earliest material date (s.9E(3)). This rule does not apply to an unpublished standard patent application (s.9E(4)). [cites: Cap. 514 9E; s.9E(3) (independent invention, earlier material date); s.9E(4) (does not apply to unpublished standard patent applications).]

## disputes
- [V] **disputes-1** Held text supports additional damages for flagrant or widespread infringement (528 s.108(2)) and delivery-up of infringing copies or equipment (s.109), which must be applied for within the s.110 period and may go to the District Court within the tort limit (s.134). Injunctions and an account of profits are not in the held Ordinance text. [cites: Cap. 528 108; Cap. 528 109; Cap. 528 134; s.108(2)(a)-(e) (factors for additional damages); s.109(1) and (2) (delivery up); s.134(1) (District Court limit).]
- [V] **disputes-2** There is no general copyright groundless-threats remedy in held Cap. 528: s.187 covers only threats about parallel-imported copies. Groundless-threat relief exists for patents (514 s.89, with the s.89A defences), registered trade marks (559 s.26) and registered designs (522 s.57). [cites: Cap. 528 187; Cap. 514 89; Cap. 559 26; Cap. 522 57; 528 s.187(1) and (3) (parallel imports; notice of copyright is not a threat); 514 s.89(1)-(3) and s.89A(1)-(2); 559 s.26(1)-(6); 522 s.57(1)-(5).]
- [V] **disputes-3** Customs and Excise enforces copyright offences through authorized officers appointed by the Commissioner of Customs and Excise. Those officers may enter and search places they reasonably suspect hold infringing copies and seize them (528 s.122). Prosecution must start within 3 years of the offence (s.120A). [cites: Cap. 528 122; Cap. 528 120A; Cap. 528 198; Cap. 362 14; 528 s.122(1)(a)-(b) and (2) (powers); s.198(1) definitions of authorized officer and Commissioner (Commissioner of Customs and Excise); s.120A (3 years); 362 s.14 (appointment for the Trade Descriptions Ordinance).]
- [V] **disputes-4** A service provider is not liable for damages for copyright infringement on its platform merely because it provides online services, if it meets the s.88B conditions: reasonable steps once aware or notified, no direct financial benefit, no interference with standard technical measures, and a designated agent. ## Review corrections
