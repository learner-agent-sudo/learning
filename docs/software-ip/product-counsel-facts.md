## R1. Names, marks and marketing claims

- **R1.1 [V]** A person with a bona fide intention to use a mark in commerce may file an intent-to-use application under 15 U.S.C. § 1051(b)(1). No mark is registered until the applicant has filed and had accepted a verified statement of use under § 1051(d), which is due within six months after the notice of allowance and can be extended under § 1051(d)(2).  
  *Source:* 15 U.S.C. § 1051(b)(1), (b)(3), (d)(1), (d)(2) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15.htm
- **R1.2 [V]** Filing an application to register is constructive use, contingent on the mark registering, and gives nationwide priority dating from the filing date. The priority does not run against a person who, before that filing, had used the mark, had a pending or registered application for it, or held a foreign-priority application that gives a right under § 1126(d).  
  *Source:* 15 U.S.C. § 1057(c), (c)(1) to (c)(3) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15.htm
- **R1.3 [V]** A registrant may give notice of registration with the words 'Registered in U.S. Patent and Trademark Office', 'Reg. U.S. Pat. & Tm. Off.' or the letter R in a circle. If a registrant sues for infringement without giving that notice, no profits and no damages are recoverable unless the defendant had actual notice of the registration. The notice is optional, and the section does not bar an injunction.  
  *Source:* 15 U.S.C. § 1111 · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15.htm
- **R1.4 [R]** Using ® with a mark that is not registered is improper (USPTO TMEP § 906.02, reported; check); fraud on the USPTO needs a specific intent to deceive (In re Bose Corp., 580 F.3d 1240 (Fed. Cir. 2009)).  
  *Source:* TMEP §§ 906.02, 906.04 (secondary); In re Bose Corp., 580 F.3d 1240 (Fed. Cir. 2009) · https://www.courtlistener.com/c/f3d/580/1240/
- **R1.5 [R]** The USPTO retired TESS (the Trademark Electronic Search System) at 11:59 p.m. ET on 30 November 2023 and replaced it with its Trademark Search system.  
  *Source:* USPTO notice, Retiring TESS: What to know about the new trademark search system (2023) · https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2023/retiring-tess-what-know-about-new-trademark-search-system
- **R1.6 [V]** Nominative fair use lets a business use another's mark to refer to that owner's product where the product is not readily identifiable without the mark, only as much of the mark as is reasonably needed is used, and nothing suggests sponsorship. The Ninth Circuit set this out in New Kids on the Block v. News America Publishing, 971 F.2d 302 (9th Cir. 1992), and applied it to domain names in Toyota Motor Sales v. Tabari, 610 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2010). Federal dilution excludes nominative and descriptive fair use, including comparative advertising (15 U.S.C. § 1125(c)(3)(A)).  
  *Source:* New Kids on the Block v. News America Publishing, 971 F.2d 302 (9th Cir. 1992); Toyota Motor Sales v. Tabari, 610 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2010); 15 U.S.C. § 1125(c)(3)(A) · https://www.courtlistener.com/c/f2d/971/302/
- **R1.7 [V]** Section 1125(a)(1)(B) makes a person liable in a civil action for misrepresenting the nature, characteristics, qualities or geographic origin of goods, services or commercial activities in commercial advertising or promotion, by anyone who believes they are or are likely to be damaged. In Lexmark International v. Static Control Components, the Supreme Court held that a plaintiff must fall within the statute's zone of interests (an injury to a commercial interest in reputation or sales) and show proximate cause.  
  *Source:* 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(B); Lexmark International, Inc. v. Static Control Components, Inc., 572 U.S. 118 (2014) (decided 25 March 2014) · https://www.courtlistener.com/c/us/572/118/
- **R1.8 [V]** Hong Kong: Trade Marks Ordinance (Cap. 559) s.94: falsely representing that a sign is a registered trade mark, knowing or having reason to believe it false, is an offence (fine at level 3); s.95 concerns misuse of the title of the Registry.  
  *Source:* Trade Marks Ordinance (Cap. 559), s.94 (secondary, from table of contents) · https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap559
- **R1.9 [R]** Under the UK Trade Marks Act 1994 section 95(1)(a), it is an offence to falsely represent a trade mark as registered. Subsection (2) treats use of 'registered' or any word or symbol that refers to registration as a representation of registration. A summary-conviction penalty of a fine not exceeding level 3 on the standard scale applies.  
  *Source:* Trade Marks Act 1994 (c.26), s.95(1)(a), (2); penalty per s.95 (summary conviction, level 3) · https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/section/95
- **R1.10 [R]** Since 17 June 2019 a Canadian trademark can be registered without first being used. An applicant can rely on use or on an intention to use in Canada, and no declaration of use or date of first use is needed for registration, though prior use still decides priority in disputes. Trademarks Act s.7(b) prohibits directing public attention to one's goods, services or business in a way likely to cause confusion, which is the statutory passing-off prohibition.  
  *Source:* Trademarks Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. T-13), s.7(b); 2019 amendments in force 17 June 2019 (secondary) · https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/T-13/

## R2. Design assets: fonts, images, interfaces, trade dress, design patents

- **R2.1 [R]** In the US a typeface as typeface is not subject to copyright (37 C.F.R. § 202.1(e)), but original computer programs that generate typefaces or fonts can be registered as literary works where they contain enough original authorship (Compendium (Third) ch. 700, § 723). The 1992 amendment to § 202.1 is cited to 57 Fed. Reg. 6202 (Feb. 21, 1992), not 6201 as the claim states.  
  *Source:* 37 C.F.R. § 202.1(a), (e) (amendment history: 57 Fed. Reg. 6202, Feb. 21, 1992); Compendium (Third) § 723 · https://www.copyright.gov/title37/202/37cfr202-1.html
- **R2.2 [V]** The SIL Open Font License 1.1 lets fonts be used, studied, modified, embedded and bundled with software, but the font software cannot be sold by itself (condition 1). Modified versions may not use a Reserved Font Name without written permission (condition 3), and modified or unmodified font software must be distributed entirely under the OFL, not another licence (condition 5). Documents made with the fonts are not subject to the licence.  
  *Source:* SIL Open Font License 1.1 (26 Feb 2007), Permission & Conditions 1), 2), 3), 5); local text held as OFL-1.1 · local licence store: scripts/law.py OFL-1.1
- **R2.3 [R]** Lotus v. Borland held that the Lotus 1-2-3 menu command hierarchy is an uncopyrightable method of operation under 17 U.S.C. § 102(b), and the Supreme Court affirmed by an equally divided Court. Apple v. Microsoft filtered out unprotectable and licensed GUI elements and gave the remaining elements only thin protection against near-verbatim copying.  
  *Source:* Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, 49 F.3d 807 (1st Cir. 1995), aff'd by equally divided Court, 516 U.S. 233 (1996); Apple Computer v. Microsoft, 35 F.3d 1435 (9th Cir. 1994) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Development_Corp._v._Borland_International,_Inc.
- **R2.4 [R]** Product-design trade dress is protectable without registration only on proof of secondary meaning (Wal-Mart v. Samara Brothers). Functional features cannot be trade dress (TrafFix). For unregistered trade dress the claimant bears the burden of proving the trade dress is not functional under 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(3).  
  *Source:* Wal-Mart Stores v. Samara Brothers, 529 U.S. 205 (2000); TrafFix Devices v. Marketing Displays, 532 U.S. 23 (2001); 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(3) · https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-supreme-court/532/23.html
- **R2.5 [R]** A design patent can protect the ornamental design of a computer-generated icon or graphical interface when it is shown embodied in an article of manufacture such as a display screen (35 U.S.C. § 171; MPEP § 1504.01(a)). The term is 15 years from grant for applications filed on or after 13 May 2015 (35 U.S.C. § 173). Since 13 March 2026, USPTO supplemental guidance no longer requires the display screen to be drawn in solid or broken lines where the title and claim already name the article, so the drawing practice in MPEP § 1504.01(a) has changed.  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. §§ 171, 173; MPEP § 1504.01(a); USPTO supplemental design patent guidance, 91 Fed. Reg. (Federal Register doc. 2026-04987, effective 13 March 2026) · https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-04987.pdf
- **R2.6 [R]** The Copyright Office's registration guidance for works containing AI-generated material requires applicants to disclose AI-generated content that is more than de minimis, to briefly explain the human author's contribution, and to exclude that AI-generated content from the claim. The guidance is a policy statement issued 16 March 2023 and published at 88 Fed. Reg. 16190.  
  *Source:* Copyright Registration Guidance: Works Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence, 88 Fed. Reg. 16190 (16 Mar. 2023) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-03-16/html/2023-05321.htm
- **R2.7 [R]** The CJEU held in Bezpečnostní softwarová asociace (C-393/09, 22 Dec 2010) that a GUI is not protected as a computer program under the Software Directive, but may be protected by copyright as a work if it is its author's own intellectual creation. In SAS Institute v World Programming (C-406/10, 2 May 2012) the Court held that the functionality of a program, its programming language and data file formats are not protected by the Software Directive, because they are ideas and principles rather than expression.  
  *Source:* Case C-393/09 Bezpečnostní softwarová asociace, judgment of 22 Dec. 2010; Case C-406/10 SAS Institute v World Programming, judgment of 2 May 2012 · https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/graphical-user-interfaces-can-be-protected-by-copyright-rules-european-court
- **R2.8 [R]** Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 amends the EU design regulation. Its new definition of product expressly includes non-physical manifestations such as graphical user interfaces, and it permits static, dynamic and animated representations. Most of the regulation has applied since 1 May 2025, although some articles that need secondary legislation apply from 1 July 2026. Computer programs remain excluded from the definition of product.  
  *Source:* Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 (amending Council Regulation (EC) No 6/2002); applicable from 1 May 2025, with some provisions from 1 July 2026 · https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2025-05/eu-design-reform-begins-key-provisions-of-eu-regulation-2024-2822-effective-from-1-may
- **R2.9 [V]** CDPA 1988 s.54 makes it not an infringement of copyright in a typeface design to use the typeface in the ordinary course of typing, composing, typesetting or printing. Section 55 deals with articles specifically designed for producing a typeface and limits later infringement by making or dealing with such articles after a period of 25 years from the end of the calendar year in which they were first marketed. In THJ Systems v Sheridan the Court of Appeal held that software-generated screen displays can be artistic works and applied the author's own intellectual creation test for originality.  
  *Source:* Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 ss.54, 55; THJ Systems Ltd v Sheridan [2023] EWCA Civ 1354 · https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2023/1354
- **R2.10 [R]** Canada's Industrial Design Office registers designs for graphical user interfaces and icons shown on a display screen. Animated designs are examined as one design, and the icon must be identified with its finished article (such as a computer monitor). Hong Kong's Registered Designs Ordinance (Cap. 522) defines design by reference to shape, configuration, pattern or ornament applied to an article by an industrial process. In practice GUIs and icons are registrable in Hong Kong only indirectly, as designs applied to screen displays, and the government consulted from 17 Dec 2025 on amending the definitions of design and article to cover transient digital designs.  
  *Source:* Canada CIPO Industrial Design Practice Notices (16 Jan 2017); Registered Designs Ordinance (Cap. 522) s.2; Hong Kong government consultation launched 17 Dec 2025 · https://clic.org.hk/en/topics/intellectualProperty/registered_designs

## R3. Third-party code, SDKs, APIs, website terms, scraping and data licences

- **R3.1 [R]** Online terms bind a user only where the site gives reasonably conspicuous notice of them and the user takes an action that unambiguously shows assent. Browsewrap terms behind a link were not enforced in Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble, and a sign-up screen with clear notice was enforced in Meyer v. Uber Technologies; Berman v. Freedom Financial Network applied the two-part test and refused to enforce terms where the notice was small and the button did not signal agreement.  
  *Source:* Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble, 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2014); Meyer v. Uber Technologies, 868 F.3d 66 (2d Cir. 2017); Berman v. Freedom Financial Network, 30 F.4th 849 (9th Cir. 2022) · https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/20-16900/20-16900-2022-04-05.html
- **R3.2 [R]** Under the CFAA a person exceeds authorized access when, although entitled to use the computer, they obtain information from areas of it, such as files, folders or databases, that are off limits to them. Misusing information they are entitled to reach, including in breach of a purpose limit, is not enough (Van Buren v. United States).  
  *Source:* Van Buren v. United States, 593 U.S. 374 (2021) · https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/593/19-783/
- **R3.3 [R]** hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn: the Ninth Circuit held in 2022 that scraping publicly available profiles was likely not access 'without authorization' under the CFAA. On remand the district court gave summary judgment to LinkedIn on the contract claim in November 2022, finding hiQ breached the User Agreement; the parties then settled in December 2022 with a consent judgment (US$500,000) and a permanent injunction, entered on 8 December 2022. The settlement terms are not judicial findings.  
  *Source:* hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn, 31 F.4th 1180 (9th Cir. 2022); N.D. Cal. summary judgment order (November 2022); consent judgment and permanent injunction (8 December 2022) · https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case
- **R3.4 [R]** In Meta Platforms v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., 23 January 2024) the court held that Meta's terms did not govern Bright Data's logged-off scraping of public data, so there was no contract breach; Meta then dropped its remaining tortious interference claim without prejudice in late February 2024. In X Corp. v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., May 2024) the court dismissed X's access-based contract claims for failure to state a claim and dismissed claims over scraping and selling public data as conflict-preempted by the Copyright Act.  
  *Source:* Meta Platforms, Inc. v. Bright Data Ltd., No. 3:23-cv-00077-EMC (N.D. Cal. 23 Jan. 2024); X Corp. v. Bright Data Ltd., No. C 23-03698 WHA (N.D. Cal. May 2024) · https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2023cv00077/406956/181
- **R3.5 [R]** ODbL 1.0 (used by OpenStreetMap from 2012) requires a Derivative Database that is Publicly Used to be offered under the ODbL or a compatible licence (s.4.4). A Produced Work, such as a rendered map image or text made from the database, needs only an attribution notice under s.4.3, but under the SPDX text, publicly using a Produced Work made from a Derivative Database also counts as publicly using that Derivative Database, so the share-alike duty can then be triggered.  
  *Source:* Open Data Commons Open Database License v1.0, ss.4.3 and 4.4 (SPDX ODbL-1.0) · https://spdx.org/licenses/ODbL-1.0.html
- **R3.6 [R]** Under Database Directive 96/9/EC Art. 7, the maker of a database that required substantial investment has a sui generis right against extraction or re-utilisation of all or a substantial part of its contents. Where neither a database right nor copyright applies, the Database Directive's user rights do not stop contractual limits on use, as held in Ryanair v PR Aviation (subject to privity of contract and national law). A specialised search engine's re-utilisation of a freely accessible database infringes the sui generis right only where it harms the maker's ability to recover its investment (CV-Online Latvia v Melons, 3 June 2021).  
  *Source:* Directive 96/9/EC, Art. 7; Case C-30/14 Ryanair v PR Aviation (CJEU, 15 January 2015); Case C-762/19 CV-Online Latvia v Melons (CJEU, 3 June 2021, ECLI:EU:C:2021:434) · https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62014CJ0030
- **R3.7 [R]** The UK database right continues for qualifying UK databases under the Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997 (SI 1997/3032) after Brexit. Canada has no sui generis database right; a compilation is protected by copyright as a work resulting from the selection or arrangement of data (Copyright Act s.2 'compilation'), with an originality threshold developed in case law, including Tele-Direct v American Business Information (FCA 1997). Hong Kong has no sui generis database right; a compilation of data that is an intellectual creation by reason of its selection or arrangement is a literary work under Cap. 528 s.4(1)(a).  
  *Source:* Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997, SI 1997/3032; Copyright Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42, s.2 ('compilation'); Tele-Direct (Publications) Inc. v. American Business Information Inc. (FCA, 1997); Copyright Ordinance (Cap. 528) s.4(1)(a) · https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-2.html
- **R3.8 [R]** Apple's Licensed Application End User License Agreement is the standard EULA for App Store apps where the developer supplies no custom terms. The Free Software Foundation's view is that App Store terms conflict with the GPL because they restrict redistribution that the GPL permits. VLC was removed from the App Store in January 2011 after a GPL complaint by a VLC contributor, though sources disagree on whether VideoLAN itself asked for removal. The FSF position is a view, not a court ruling.  
  *Source:* Apple Licensed Application End User License Agreement (standard EULA); VLC for iOS removal from the App Store (January 2011) · https://www.engadget.com/2011-01-08-vlc-app-removed-from-app-store.html
- **R3.9 [R]** In Google LLC v. Oracle America (2021), a 6-2 Court held that Google's copying of about 11,500 lines of Java SE declaring code was fair use, and it assumed without deciding that the declaring code was copyrightable.  
  *Source:* Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc., 593 U.S. 1 (2021) · https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/593/18-956/
- **R3.10 [R]** Google Maps Platform terms restrict caching: customers may not cache Google Maps Content except as the Maps Service Specific Terms expressly permit, for example temporary caching of geocode latitude and longitude for up to 30 consecutive days, while place IDs may be stored indefinitely. Product counsel should read SDK and API terms before the SDK ships, because such terms usually restrict use and data handling and the provider can change or end them.  
  *Source:* Google Maps Platform Terms of Service (EEA version, s.3.2.3(b) on caching; another copy numbers the misuse restrictions 3.3.2); Maps Service Specific Terms (Geocoding API caching) · https://cloud.google.com/archive/terms/maps-platform/eea-20250604

## R4. Patents in product work

- **R4.1 [V]** Under 35 U.S.C. § 102(b)(1)(A), a disclosure made one year or less before the effective filing date by the inventor, or by someone who got the subject matter directly or indirectly from the inventor, is not prior art under § 102(a)(1). The US therefore gives a one-year grace period for the inventor's own disclosures, subject to the conditions in § 102(b)(1)(B).  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)(1), (b)(1)(A)-(B) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title35/html/USCODE-2024-title35.htm
- **R4.2 [R]** A provisional application needs no claims: § 111(b)(2) says a claim is not required, and the application is regarded as abandoned 12 months after its filing date unless converted (§ 111(b)(5)). Under § 119(e)(1), a later non-provisional application that claims the benefit of the provisional gets the provisional's filing date for an invention disclosed as required by § 112(a), provided it is filed within 12 months.  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. § 111(b)(2), (b)(5); § 119(e)(1) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title35/html/USCODE-2024-title35.htm
- **R4.3 [R]** EPC Art. 54 applies absolute novelty. Under Art. 55(1), a disclosure during the six months before filing is disregarded only if it resulted from an evident abuse in relation to the applicant (or legal predecessor), or from display at an official or officially recognised international exhibition under the 1928 Paris Convention. Art. 55(2) requires a declaration on filing and a certificate for the exhibition case.  
  *Source:* European Patent Convention Art. 54, Art. 55(1)-(2) · https://www.ippt.eu/legal-texts/european-patent-convention/article-55
- **R4.4 [R]** UK Patents Act 1977 s.2(4) also disregards disclosures made within six months before filing, but it is not a word-for-word mirror of EPC Art. 55. It covers disclosures of matter obtained unlawfully or in breach of confidence (s.2(4)(a) and (b)) and displays at an international exhibition under s.2(4)(c), which requires a statement on filing and written evidence within the prescribed period. The 'evident abuse' wording of Art. 55 is not used in the same terms.  
  *Source:* Patents Act 1977 s.2(4)(a)-(c) · https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/37/section/2
- **R4.5 [V]** Canada: Patent Act s.28.2(1)(a): a disclosure by the applicant, or by someone who learned of it from the applicant, is prior art only if made before the one-year period before the filing date, so the applicant's own disclosures in the year before filing do not count.  
  *Source:* Patent Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. P-4) s.28.2(1)(a) · https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-4/section-28.2.html
- **R4.6 [V]** Hong Kong has three non-prejudicial disclosure provisions, each with the same two conditions. Section 11A covers standard (R) re-registration applications, s.37B covers standard (O) applications, and s.109 covers short-term patent applications. Each disregards a disclosure made no earlier than 6 months before the filing date if it was due to an evident abuse against the applicant or proprietor, or to display at an official or officially recognised international exhibition under the 1928 Paris Convention. The exhibition route needs a statement on filing and prescribed written evidence (s.11A(3), s.37B(3), s.109).  
  *Source:* Patents Ordinance (Cap. 514) s.9B, s.11A(1)-(3), s.37B(1)-(3), s.109 · https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap514
- **R4.7 [R]** In Halo Electronics v. Pulse Electronics, 579 U.S. 93 (2016), the Supreme Court rejected the rigid Seagate test. District courts have discretion under 35 U.S.C. § 284 to award enhanced damages (up to three times) in egregious cases of culpable, typically subjective, willful infringement. Enhancement needs no objective-recklessness threshold, and willfulness is proved by a preponderance of the evidence.  
  *Source:* Halo Electronics, Inc. v. Pulse Electronics, Inc., 579 U.S. 93 (2016); 35 U.S.C. § 284 · https://www.oyez.org/cases/2015/14-1513
- **R4.8 [V]** Under 35 U.S.C. § 298, an infringer's failure to obtain advice of counsel, or to present it to the court or jury, may not be used to prove wilful infringement or intent to induce infringement. Relying on an opinion can still waive privilege, and a court may still consider the omission when deciding whether to enhance damages once wilfulness is found.  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. § 298; Seagate (Fed. Cir. 2007, en banc) as background · https://law.lawstack.com/usc/35/298/
- **R4.9 [V]** Under 35 U.S.C. § 287(a), a patentee may give notice by marking the article with the word 'patent' or 'pat.' and the patent number, or with 'patent' or 'pat.' and an internet address of a free public posting that associates the article with the patent number. If the patentee fails to mark, damages are not recoverable for infringement before the infringer was notified of the infringement and kept infringing, and filing an infringement action counts as notice. The marking rule covers articles claimed in the patent, not method claims alone.  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. § 287(a) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title35/html/USCODE-2024-title35.htm
- **R4.10 [V]** Under 35 U.S.C. § 292(a), only the United States may sue for the fine of up to $500 per offence for false marking. Under § 292(b), a person who has suffered a competitive injury may sue in federal district court for damages adequate to compensate. Under § 292(c), marking a product with a patent that has expired is not a violation. The Leahy-Smith America Invents Act of 2011 made the amendments that produced this structure.  
  *Source:* 35 U.S.C. § 292(a)-(c); Pub. L. 112-29, § 16(b) · https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title35/html/USCODE-2024-title35.htm

## R5. Launch: notices, takedown agents, user content, feedback and app stores

- **R5.1 [V]** A copyright notice is optional for works published on or after 1 March 1989, but if a proper notice appears on the published copies, an infringer cannot rely on innocent infringement to reduce actual or statutory damages (17 U.S.C. § 401(d)).  
  *Source:* 17 U.S.C. § 401(a), (d); see also 17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) · https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/401
- **R5.2 [R]** A service provider qualifies for the § 512(c) storage safe harbour only if it has designated an agent to receive infringement notices with the Copyright Office. Electronic designations expire three years after registration unless renewed by amendment or by resubmitting them unchanged, and a lapsed designation loses the safe harbour.  
  *Source:* 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2); 37 C.F.R. § 201.38 · https://www.copyright.gov/title37/201/37cfr201-38.html
- **R5.3 [R]** 47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(2) says Section 230 does not limit or expand any law pertaining to intellectual property, so it gives no general shield against IP claims. Circuits differ on state-law IP claims: the Ninth Circuit reads the carve-out as federal IP only (Perfect 10 v. CCBill, 488 F.3d 1102 (9th Cir. 2007)), while the Third Circuit held that a state right of publicity claim falls within the carve-out and is not immunised (Hepp v. Facebook, 3d Cir. 2021).  
  *Source:* 47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(2); Perfect 10, Inc. v. CCBill LLC, 488 F.3d 1102 (9th Cir. 2007); Hepp v. Facebook (3d Cir. Sept. 2021) · https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2021/09/third-circuit-says-section-230-doesnt-apply-to-publicity-rights-claims-hepp-v-facebook.htm
- **R5.4 [R]** Under California law, a person who discloses an idea to a recipient who accepts it knowing the discloser expects payment can recover on an implied-in-fact contract if the idea is used, even where the idea is not copyrightable (Desny v. Wilder, 46 Cal. 2d 715 (1956)). A feedback clause that licenses suggestions to the company is a common way to manage this risk.  
  *Source:* Desny v. Wilder, 46 Cal. 2d 715, 299 P.2d 257 (Cal. 1956) · https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/desny-v-wilder-26737
- **R5.5 [V]** Apple's App Review Guideline 5.2 (Intellectual Property) says apps should include only content the developer created or has a licence to use. Guideline 5.2.1 (Generally) says not to use protected third-party material, such as trademarks, copyrighted works or patented ideas, in an app without permission, and that apps should be submitted by the entity that owns or licenses the IP.

- **R5.6 DROP**: could not be confirmed (37 C.F.R. § 202.20(c)(2)(vii); Copyright Office Circular 61).
- **R5.7 [R]** Article 16 of the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) requires hosting providers to run easy-to-use notice and action mechanisms for illegal content, which includes IP-infringing content. Providers must act on notices in a timely way and tell the notifier whether they decided to act. The obligation has applied to all hosting services since 17 February 2024.  
  *Source:* Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, Art. 16 (and Art. 3(h) on illegal content) · https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/
- **R5.8 [R]** Canada's notice-and-notice regime (Copyright Act ss. 41.25 to 41.27) requires an internet intermediary to forward a copyright owner's notice of claimed infringement to the user and to tell the claimant it was forwarded, without a duty to remove content.
