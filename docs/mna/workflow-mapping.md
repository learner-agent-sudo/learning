# Workflow mapping — APA, 9 stages

Each stage below lists:

- **Description** — what happens, at two depths (Beginner / Practitioner).
- **Documents produced** — the artifacts a deal team creates.
- **Clauses** — provisions typically appearing in stage documents, mapped to
  CUAD categories where possible. Items marked **[gap-fill]** are
  hand-authored because CUAD under-represents them in an APA context.
- **Glossary** — terms introduced or made central at this stage.

CUAD has 41 labeled clause categories. The mapping below cites them by their
canonical CUAD names (e.g., "Governing Law", "Anti-Assignment").

---

## Stage 1 — Strategy *(pre-process, dashed)*

**Beginner.** Before any deal exists, the buyer decides *why* it wants to
acquire something — new customers, new tech, new geography — and the seller
decides *why* it wants to sell. No documents are signed with a counterparty
yet; this stage is internal.

**Practitioner.** Buy-side: investment thesis, target screen, valuation
range, financing plan, board approval to pursue. Sell-side: sale rationale,
process design (auction vs. bilateral), banker engagement, vendor due
diligence prep.

**Documents produced**
- Internal investment memo / board deck
- Engagement letter with financial advisor (sell-side or buy-side banker)
- Confidential information memorandum (CIM) — sell-side
- Process letter — sell-side, in an auction

**Clauses**
- Engagement letter: fee structure, tail period, exclusivity, indemnification
  of the banker. *(CUAD: Exclusivity, Cap on Liability — partial match.)*
- CIM: legal disclaimer, no-reliance language. **[gap-fill]**

**Glossary**
- *Buy-side / sell-side* — which party the advisor represents.
- *CIM* — confidential information memorandum, the sell-side marketing book.
- *Tail period* — window after engagement ends during which the banker still
  earns a fee if the client closes with an introduced buyer.

---

## Stage 2 — NDA (Confidentiality Agreement)

**Beginner.** Before the seller shares sensitive information, both sides
sign an NDA promising not to leak it or use it for anything other than
evaluating the deal.

**Practitioner.** One-way (seller → buyer) or mutual. Key negotiation
points: definition of confidential information, term, permitted uses,
non-solicitation of employees, standstill (in public-target contexts),
residuals clause, return/destruction obligations, governing law.

**Documents produced**
- Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) / Confidentiality Agreement

**Clauses (CUAD-mapped)**
- *Governing Law* — CUAD category.
- *No-Solicit Of Employees* — CUAD category. Common 12–24 month tail.
- *Non-Disparagement* — CUAD category (sometimes).
- *Cap On Liability* — CUAD category.
- Standstill **[gap-fill]** — restricts buyer from buying seller's stock or
  launching a hostile bid; CUAD has it implicitly but not as a labeled
  category.
- Residuals carve-out **[gap-fill]** — what the buyer's people are allowed
  to "remember."

**Glossary**
- *Standstill* — buyer agrees not to make unsolicited offers for a period.
- *Residuals* — unaided memory of confidential info; a contested NDA term.

---

## Stage 3 — LOI / Term Sheet *(deal-type branch point)*

**Beginner.** The two sides write down the headline deal terms — price,
structure, timeline, exclusivity — in a short document that is **mostly
non-binding**. This is the first time the deal becomes "real."

**Practitioner.** Letter of intent (or term sheet) sets price/range,
consideration mix (cash/stock/earnout), structure (**asset / stock /
merger**), key conditions, exclusivity (no-shop) period, expense
responsibility. Typically: non-binding except for exclusivity, confidentiality,
expenses, governing law, and termination.

> **Branch point:** structure choice here drives the rest of the workflow.
> v1 of this site shows the **APA path** in colour; **SPA** and **Merger**
> branches are greyed out with "coming soon" tooltips.

**Documents produced**
- Letter of Intent (LOI) / Term Sheet / MOU
- Process bid letter (auction context)

**Clauses (CUAD-mapped + gap-fill)**
- *Exclusivity* — CUAD category. Typical 30–60 days, no-shop + no-talk.
- *Expense Reimbursement* — CUAD-adjacent. Each side bears own expenses,
  except break-fee carve-outs.
- *Governing Law* — CUAD category.
- Binding vs. non-binding split **[gap-fill]** — APA-specific drafting:
  recital language naming which sections survive.
- Headline price + consideration mix **[gap-fill]** — economic terms.
- Conditions to definitive agreement **[gap-fill]** — DD, financing, board.

**Glossary**
- *No-shop / no-talk* — seller can't solicit / can't even respond to other
  bidders.
- *Earnout* — portion of price contingent on post-closing performance.
- *Asset vs. stock vs. merger* — three structures. APA = asset; SPA = stock;
  merger = entity-level combination.

## Stage 4 — Due Diligence

**Beginner.** Buyer's lawyers, accountants, and specialists pore over the
seller's documents to confirm the business is what the seller says it is
and to find risks that should change the price or the contract terms.

**Practitioner.** Workstreams: legal (corporate, contracts, litigation, IP,
employment, regulatory, real estate, environmental), financial (quality of
earnings), tax, commercial, technical/IT, HR, ESG. Output is a DD report
with risk flags that flow into APA reps, indemnities, and closing
conditions. v1 of this site treats DD as a single node; sub-streams are a
v2 enhancement.

**Documents produced**
- Data room index
- Due diligence request list
- DD reports (legal, financial, tax, commercial)
- Quality of Earnings (QoE) report
- Issues list / red-flag memo
- Disclosure schedule drafts (seller-prepared, against APA reps)

**Clauses (CUAD-mapped, surfaced from reviewed contracts)**
- *Change of Control* — CUAD category. Triggers in target's customer/vendor
  contracts.
- *Anti-Assignment* — CUAD category. **Critical in APA**: if a contract bars
  assignment, buyer needs counterparty consent at closing.
- *Most Favored Nation* — CUAD category. Pricing risk in customer contracts.
- *Exclusivity* — CUAD category. Found in target's existing contracts.
- *Non-Compete* — CUAD category.
- *Termination For Convenience* — CUAD category.
- *Audit Rights* — CUAD category.
- *Insurance* — CUAD category.
- *IP Ownership Assignment* — CUAD category.
- *License Grant* — CUAD category.

**Glossary**
- *Data room* — secure online repository of seller's documents.
- *QoE* — Quality of Earnings, an accounting deep-dive on EBITDA.
- *Disclosure schedule* — exceptions list to the seller's reps and warranties.
- *Anti-assignment* — contract clause blocking transfer to a new owner.

## Stage 5 — APA Drafting & Negotiation

**Beginner.** The lawyers write the long contract — the Asset Purchase
Agreement — that says exactly which assets and liabilities transfer, for
what price, and what happens if something goes wrong.

**Practitioner.** Buyer's counsel typically drafts first in the U.S. The
APA contains: definitions, purchase/sale of assets and assumed liabilities,
purchase price + adjustments, closing mechanics, reps and warranties (R&W)
of seller and buyer, pre-closing covenants, conditions to closing,
termination rights, indemnification, and miscellaneous (governing law,
notices, etc.). Negotiation centers on R&W scope, indemnity caps/baskets/
survival, MAC definition, and purchase-price adjustment.

**Documents produced**
- Asset Purchase Agreement (APA)
- Disclosure schedules
- Schedules of assumed/excluded assets and assumed/excluded liabilities
- Form ancillary agreements (bill of sale, assignment & assumption,
  IP assignment, transition services, employment/non-compete)

**Clauses — APA-specific, CUAD-mapped where applicable**
- *Governing Law* — CUAD.
- *Anti-Assignment* — CUAD. Mirror in the APA: APA itself's assignability.
- *Cap On Liability* — CUAD. Indemnification cap.
- *Notice Period To Terminate Renewal* — CUAD-adjacent.
- *Insurance* — CUAD. R&W insurance increasingly standard.
- *IP Ownership Assignment* — CUAD. Mechanics for transferring IP at close.
- *License Grant* — CUAD. Cross-licenses where seller retains some IP.
- *Non-Compete* — CUAD. Seller covenant not to compete post-closing.
- *No-Solicit Of Employees / Customers* — CUAD.

**APA-specific gap-fill clauses (hand-authored)**
- **Purchased Assets / Excluded Assets** — defines exactly what transfers.
- **Assumed Liabilities / Excluded Liabilities** — the corresponding split.
- **Purchase Price & Adjustment** — working-capital, debt, cash, tax true-up.
- **Earnout** — contingent consideration with measurement and dispute
  mechanics.
- **Escrow / Holdback** — portion of price held back to fund indemnity.
- **Representations & Warranties (Seller)** — organization, authority,
  no-conflict, financial statements, no undisclosed liabilities, taxes,
  litigation, compliance, contracts, IP, employees, benefits, environmental,
  real property, sufficiency of assets, customers/suppliers, brokers.
- **Representations & Warranties (Buyer)** — organization, authority,
  financing, no-conflict.
- **Pre-Closing Covenants** — operate in ordinary course, access,
  no-solicit of other buyers, regulatory cooperation.
- **Conditions to Closing** — bring-down of reps, no MAC, regulatory
  clearance, third-party consents, ancillary deliveries.
- **Indemnification** — survival periods, basket (deductible/tipping),
  cap, exclusions (fraud, fundamental reps), exclusive remedy, sandbagging.
- **Material Adverse Change (MAC)** — definition + carve-outs.
- **Termination & Termination Fee** — drop-dead date, breach, mutual.
- **Tax Matters** — allocation under §1060, transfer taxes, cooperation.
- **Bulk Sales** — usually waived in modern APAs.
- **Non-Compete & Non-Solicit (Seller)** — usually 2–5 years, geography-bound.
- **Employee Matters** — which employees transfer, comparable benefits.

**Glossary**
- *Reps and warranties* — statements of fact each side stands behind.
- *Basket / cap / survival* — the three knobs of indemnification scope.
- *MAC / MAE* — material adverse change/effect; closing condition shield.
- *Sandbagging* — buyer can sue on a known breach (pro-sandbag) or can't
  (anti-sandbag).
- *§1060 allocation* — IRS-required allocation of purchase price across
  asset classes.
- *R&W insurance* — third-party policy covering breach of seller's reps.

## Stage 6 — Signing

**Beginner.** Both sides sign the APA. In many deals, **signing and
closing are not the same day** — there's a gap to get regulatory approvals
and consents.

**Practitioner.** Authority confirmed via board/manager resolutions.
Signature pages exchanged, often via DocuSign with a release email.
8-K filed if buyer is U.S. public. Public announcement coordinated.
Ancillary agreements typically signed at closing, not signing, but may be
attached as forms.

**Documents produced**
- Executed APA + signature pages
- Board / member / shareholder resolutions
- Press release (if applicable)
- 8-K (if buyer is public)

**Clauses**
- Effectiveness / counterparts / electronic signature **[gap-fill]**.
- Public announcement clause **[gap-fill]** — coordinated press only.

**Glossary**
- *Signing vs. closing* — execution date vs. effective transfer date.
- *Counterparts* — separately signed pages forming one agreement.

## Stage 7 — Pre-Closing

**Beginner.** Between signing and closing, both sides have homework. The
buyer files antitrust paperwork, the seller chases consents from key
customers and lenders, and the seller has to keep running the business
"normally" — no big changes.

**Practitioner.** HSR filing (if thresholds met) and 30-day waiting period;
foreign merger filings; CFIUS (if applicable). Third-party consents:
landlords, key customers, lenders. Seller operates per ordinary-course
covenant. Buyer arranges financing draw. Bring-down certificates prepared.

**Documents produced**
- HSR filing (Hart-Scott-Rodino)
- Foreign antitrust filings
- Third-party consent letters
- Estoppel certificates
- Closing checklist (lawyer-maintained)
- Officer's bring-down certificate (draft)

**Clauses (APA covenants + CUAD-mapped underlying contracts)**
- *Anti-Assignment* — CUAD. Drives the consent campaign.
- *Change of Control* — CUAD. Same.
- Ordinary-course covenant **[gap-fill]** — APA pre-closing operating
  restrictions list.
- Regulatory cooperation covenant **[gap-fill]** — efforts standard
  ("reasonable best efforts" vs. "hell-or-high-water").
- Financing covenant **[gap-fill]** — buyer's obligation if financing-out
  was negotiated away.

**Glossary**
- *HSR* — Hart-Scott-Rodino premerger antitrust filing in the U.S.
- *CFIUS* — Committee on Foreign Investment in the U.S.; foreign buyer
  national-security review.
- *Estoppel* — counterparty confirming current state of a contract.
- *Hell-or-high-water* — strongest regulatory-efforts standard.

---

## Stage 8 — Closing

**Beginner.** The day the deal actually happens. Money moves, ownership
of the assets transfers, and a stack of ancillary documents gets signed.

**Practitioner.** Closing is mechanical execution of the APA: deliveries
on each side per the closing checklist, funds flow per the funds-flow memo,
title to assets passes via bill of sale and assignment instruments, IP
transfers via IP assignments (recorded post-close), employees onboard to
buyer, and the parties exchange officers' bring-down certificates.

**Documents produced**
- Bill of Sale
- Assignment and Assumption Agreement (contracts and liabilities)
- Intellectual Property Assignment(s) — patents, trademarks, copyrights,
  domains
- Real estate transfer documents (deeds, lease assignments)
- Transition Services Agreement (TSA)
- Employment / consulting agreements with key employees
- Non-competition / non-solicitation agreements with founders
- Escrow Agreement
- Funds Flow memo
- Officer's certificates (bring-down of reps, conditions met)
- Secretary's certificate (resolutions, incumbency)
- Legal opinion (sometimes)
- FIRPTA certificate (U.S. tax)

**Clauses (CUAD-mapped + gap-fill)**
- *IP Ownership Assignment* — CUAD. Core APA closing mechanic.
- *License Grant* — CUAD. Used in TSA cross-licenses.
- *Insurance* — CUAD. Tail policies for pre-closing exposures.
- TSA scope, term, fees, IT separation **[gap-fill]**.
- Escrow release mechanics **[gap-fill]**.
- FIRPTA certificate **[gap-fill]** — U.S. tax form to avoid withholding.

**Glossary**
- *Bill of sale* — conveys tangible personal property.
- *Assignment & assumption* — conveys contracts (and liabilities).
- *TSA* — Transition Services Agreement; seller helps buyer run the
  business for a period.
- *Funds flow* — the wire-by-wire money-movement schedule for closing.
- *FIRPTA* — Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act.

## Stage 9 — Post-Closing

**Beginner.** The deal isn't over at closing. There's a price true-up a
few months later, an indemnification window where the buyer can claim
back money for problems it discovers, and the messy work of integrating
the acquired business.

**Practitioner.** Working-capital true-up: buyer delivers post-close
statement, seller has dispute window, accountant resolves disputes,
escrow releases or top-ups. Indemnification claims under R&W survival
periods (general 12–24 months; fundamental reps longer; tax until SOL;
fraud carve-out). Earnout measurement and disputes. Integration:
systems, payroll, branding, customer comms. Post-closing covenants:
non-compete, non-solicit, further-assurances, books and records access.

**Documents produced**
- Post-closing working capital / purchase price adjustment statement
- Indemnification claim notices
- Escrow release instructions
- Earnout statements
- Post-closing tax filings (allocation, transfer tax, final returns)
- Integration plan
- Form 8594 (asset acquisition statement, U.S. tax)

**Clauses**
- *Non-Compete* — CUAD.
- *No-Solicit Of Customers / Employees* — CUAD.
- *Audit Rights* — CUAD. Used for earnout measurement.
- *Cap On Liability* — CUAD. Indemnity cap binds here.
- Working-capital adjustment mechanics **[gap-fill]**.
- Earnout dispute resolution **[gap-fill]**.
- Survival schedule **[gap-fill]** — table of which reps survive how long.
- Further assurances **[gap-fill]**.
- Books and records access **[gap-fill]**.

**Glossary**
- *True-up* — post-closing adjustment to purchase price.
- *Survival* — how long a rep can be sued on after closing.
- *Form 8594* — IRS asset-allocation form filed by both parties.
- *Further assurances* — catch-all promise to sign whatever else is needed.
